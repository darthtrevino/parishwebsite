We belong to the [Orthodox Church in America]({{ site.jurisdiction.url }}), one of the world's
autocephalous (or "self-governing") national Orthodox jurisdictions that maintains sacramental unity
with all others, such as the Russian, Greek, and Antiochian churches. We are the first Orthodox
parish of Kitsap County, having been planted as a mission by Seattle's Saint Spiridon Cathedral
in 2001. After years of borrowing space from other local churches and renting a storefront, we now
own a permanent, two-acre property just north of central Poulsbo off of Highway 3. Over the past
several years, we have undertaken several renovation projects, making our temple the beautiful
worship space it is today.

## A community of many backgrounds

Many of our members are converts to the faith, though some have been raised in it. We come from a
diversity of social, economic, and ethnic backgrounds, reminding us of Saint Paul's statement that
"there is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female;
for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (Gal. 3:28). What is common to all our members, though, is a
commitment to unity in the faith.

## Unity in the faith

Unity is cherished by our parish and is secured by our common commitment to the Orthodox faith.
While we are a socially diverse community, in the matter of faith we enjoy complete unanimity. "Let
us love one another," we declare at every Divine Liturgy, "that with one mind we may confess Father,
Son, and Holy Spirit: The Trinity, one in essence and undivided." We are one because God is one. And
we are undivided because God, existing in three persons, is undivided.

Each of our members is committed to supporting one another lovingly in the faith as it was given
once and for all by God to the Apostles and has been handed down unchanged for twenty centuries.
Questions of doctrine, morality, and worship can divide many non-Orthodox Christians today, but for
us they have long been resolved by our saintly predecessors in the faith&mdash;men and women who
worked them out in the ancient councils of the Church. We do not suffer divisions within our parish
in the face of modern innovations to Christianity or controversies in the surrounding culture. Our
people, guided by our bishop and the clergy he assigns to serve us, humbly hold fast to what we have
been given: the faith of the Apostles.

Every year on the first Sunday of Lent, known as the Sunday of Orthodoxy, we as a community
commemorate this fact during an outdoor procession when we proclaim, repeating the words of our
ninth-century forefathers, that it is this very faith that has "established the universe."
